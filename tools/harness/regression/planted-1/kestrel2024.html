How Small Can a Student Be? Temperature and Target Choice in Knowledge Distillation

Ana Kestrel, Ilya Morrow

Workshop on Efficient Learning, 2024

Abstract

We revisit knowledge distillation on Q-Bench, an image classification benchmark, and vary the size of the student, the distillation temperature, and the training targets. Small students recover most of the teacher's accuracy, the best temperature is lower than commonly assumed, and soft targets alone can be enough.

1 Introduction

Knowledge distillation trains a small student network to match the output distribution of a larger, already trained teacher. Most practitioners pick a high temperature and mix soft targets with hard labels. We ask whether these defaults hold when the student is very small.

2 Setup

Q-Bench contains 120,000 training images in 300 classes, with a held-out validation split of 15,000 images. The teacher is a 24-layer convolutional network. Students have 4, 6 or 8 layers. The teacher is trained once and kept frozen during distillation. We report top-1 accuracy on the validation split, averaged over three seeds.

3 Results

3.1 Student size

On the Q-Bench validation split, a 6-layer student with 23% of the teacher's parameters retained 91.2% of the teacher's top-1 accuracy. The 4-layer student retained 84.7%.

3.2 Temperature

We swept the temperature from 1 to 8. Student accuracy peaked at a temperature of 1.5; raising the temperature to 4 or above reduced accuracy, by 1.3 points at T = 4 compared with T = 1.5.

3.3 Targets

Training on soft targets alone outperformed the mixed objective with hard labels by 0.8 points. We attribute this to label noise in Q-Bench, which the hard labels pass on to the student.

3.4 Cost

Distillation required about 1.6 times the GPU hours of training the same student on hard labels alone, mainly because of teacher inference over the transfer set.

4 Conclusion

For very small students on Q-Bench, a low temperature and soft targets alone worked best. These findings are specific to our setting and should be re-checked on other data.
